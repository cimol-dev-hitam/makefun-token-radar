Why the family bands differ. A lending book or a DEX is fee-earning capital that a token could take a share of, so it capitalises near or above its TVL. A curator’s or an aggregator’s TVL is other people’s money in transit, so it capitalises at a discount. Perps clear the most fee flow per dollar of TVL and take the highest band.

Why momentum is asymmetric. Seven days of growth is a thin signal: it can add at most 25% (a 50% week) and take away at most 30% (a 60% week), and it is clamped there, so a single violent week cannot decide a whole rank. A protocol whose record carries no seven-day change is scored as flat.

Why age discounts. A protocol tracked for three years that still records no token has had its window; one tracked for three months has not. The discount runs from 1.10× at listing down to 0.80× after 1460 days, and a protocol with no listing date counts 540 days.

What is left out. A wrapped, staked, bridged or versioned product of a token that already trades is no launch of its own, so CEX, bridges, liquid staking and restaking, restaked BTC, versioned pools, exchange-branded pools and names carrying a whole word wrapped, staked or staking never reach the board. The protocols whose token already trades are listed in their own tab with the market’s numbers, never the model’s.
